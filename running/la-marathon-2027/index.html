WHAT PACE IS MINE?

Two tests set the goal: a 5k on Wed Dec 16 (wk 6) and a 10k on Wed Jan 20 (wk 11). The 10k counts more.
How it converts. MP = 10k pace × 1.13, or 5k pace × 1.20. That is a deliberately cautious conversion for three runs a week; the textbook ratios assume more mileage than this. Run the tests flat and evenly paced; a sprint finish doesn't count.

What to do with it. 15+ min inside the cutoff: race at MP and start with the pace team at or just under your finish. 5–15 min: run the first half 5 s/km slower than MP and walk only at aid stations. Under 5 min, or outside: tell me. Wednesdays get more MP work, the race becomes a run/walk, and the 6:30 pace team becomes the wall you stay ahead of.

BULLETPROOF THE RIGHT FOOT

Mid-portion Achilles · posterior tibialis · plantar fascia · all on the ToePro · the FOOT block on Mon/Wed/Fri · tap an exercise name for how
Where you are (Nov 12). Calf hold 3×40s, band inversion 3×10, and the single-leg heel raise coming back. Your last loaded raises were in Joshua Tree wk 22, three weeks ago, so they restart at bodyweight for two weeks and pick up the dumbbells from wk 3.

The week stays as it was: heavy straight-knee work on Monday, bent-knee and springy work on Wednesday, holds only on long-run Friday. Heavy days sit 48 h or more apart and never land on long-run legs.

The rules. (1) Each exercise goes up by at most 5% a week. For the raises the load is your body weight plus the dumbbell, so each 5-lb step adds about 2.5% (at ~86 kg). The first step, bodyweight → 10 lb, is about 5%. Once you're at 25 lb, the next step is one more rep on one set (9/8/8, then 9/9/8…), which is also under 5%. (2) Load only goes up after seven calm mornings. If not, repeat the week. (3) Cutback and test weeks drop a set or skip, and the following week is measured against the last full week. (4) The taper unloads from wk 15.

3×8 = 3 sets of 8 · 9/8/8 = reps per set · raises are per leg, dumbbell in the hand on the working side · tempo 3 s up · 1 s hold · 3 s down · rest 60–90 s.

What the evidence says, and what it doesn't.

Heavy, slow raises work. In Beyer et al. (2015, Am J Sports Med), heavy slow resistance (3 s up, 3 s down, three times a week for 12 weeks, getting heavier as reps dropped from 15 to 6) fixed Achilles tendinopathy as well as the classic eccentric protocol, and more people stuck with it. The heel raise and bent-knee raise here follow that pattern.

Tendons need high load, held long, for months. A meta-analysis of tendon training (Bohm, Mersmann & Arampatzis 2015, Sports Med Open) found the tendon gets stiffer and stronger with high loads (~85–90% of max), long contractions (~3 s), and programmes of 12 weeks or more. Light, quick reps do little. That's why the reps are slow and the dumbbell keeps climbing for the whole block.

Tendons need recovery days. After heavy loading, collagen breakdown outpaces new collagen for roughly the first day or two, and the net gain comes after that (Magnusson, Langberg & Kjaer 2010, Nat Rev Rheumatol). Hence heavy days 48 h or more apart.

The pain rule is tested. Silbernagel et al. (2007, Am J Sports Med): pain up to 5/10 that settles by the next morning is safe to keep training through, running included.

The 5% cap is not tested. No trial has compared weekly percentages for calf loading, just as none has proven the 10% running rule. It's a deliberately conservative choice, slower than the HSR trial raised its load, because the runs load the same tendon. The trials also loaded three times a week; here it's twice (Mon and Wed) plus the runs.

The exercises — exactly how

Calf hold Primer · tendon stiffness

Both feet on the ToePro, toes up. Rise about halfway and hold dead still for the count: no bouncing, no lowering. A real effort, pain ≤3/10. It's the pre-run primer and the only foot work on long-run Fridays and in race week.

Heel raise · single leg, straight knee Achilles (mid-portion) + plantar fascia · Monday

Ball of the foot on the ToePro so the toes sit higher than the heel. Dumbbell in the hand on the working side, other hand on a wall for balance only. Rise (3 s) straight up over the big toe, without letting the ankle roll outward, hold (1 s), then lower (3 s) until the heel drops below the ramp. If the dumbbell makes the single-leg rise ugly, rise on two and lower on one: the slow lower is the part that counts.

Bent-knee raise · single leg Soleus · Wednesday

The same raise on the ToePro, but with the knee bent about 30° the whole time: a shallow quarter-squat that doesn't change through the rep. The bent knee slackens the gastrocnemius and hands the work to the soleus, the deep calf muscle that takes several times your body weight on every stride, tires late in a marathon and protects the Achilles on descents.

Band inversion Posterior tibialis — below the inner ankle · Monday

Sit with the leg out and loop the band around the forefoot, anchored so it pulls the foot outward. Keep the heel still and turn the sole inward and down (2 s), then come back with control (3 s). Late in a marathon, a tired posterior tibialis lets the arch drop. That's what this prevents.

Pogos The tendon as a spring · Wednesday

Small, fast hops on the spot off the balls of both feet: stiff ankles, minimal knee bend, quick contacts, like skipping rope. The number is total contacts. New for you, so they start in wk 5, once the heel raise is at 20 lb (jumping comes after a strength base), at just 20 contacts, and grow by one a week. Double-leg only this block.

Pre-run ritual — ~7 min, before every run

1 · Calf-hold primer. 2×40s holds right before heading out.
2 · Walk 5 minutes briskly before any running.
3 · First km flat and deliberately slow. Hills only once warm, uphills before downhills.
4 · Shoes. 8–12 mm drop, retired past ~600 km. This block is ~580 km on its own: start it in a fresh pair. Buy race shoes by mid-January, so they've done the wk-10 rehearsal and at least one more long run before Mar 7.
5 · No calf stretching before runs. The holds are the warm-up.
Daily check. Rate the first steps out of bed: calm / stiff / painful. A drift upward means hold the next progression, not stop.
Pain rule. Up to 5/10 during the exercise or run is OK if it's back to normal by the next morning and isn't getting worse week to week. If morning pain climbs, you limp, or it sharpens, drop back one level (less weight / fewer sets / shorter run). Don't push through. A marathon block forgives a lost week; it doesn't forgive a lost month.

Pacing

  • The first mile drops steeply. Brake and save the quads.
  • First 10k at MP + 10 s/km. The course rolls all the way, so don't bank time in the first six miles.
  • Hold MP to 30k, then use what's left.
  • Pace teams run 3:00 → 6:30 in 10-minute steps. Start with the one that matches your test, and stay well ahead of 6:30.

Fuel & drink

  • One 30 g gel every 30 min from minute 30 (~60 g/h, about 11 gels over six hours).
  • The course's gels at miles 12 and 18 are top-ups. Carry the rest.
  • Water and Electrolit every ~1.5 miles: walk 15–20 s, drink, go. That's the rhythm you rehearsed.

If the Achilles talks

  • Switch to run 4 min / walk 1 min and finish it that way.
  • The pain rule doesn't change on race day.
  • After: keep walking, eat within the hour, and walk only for the next week.
