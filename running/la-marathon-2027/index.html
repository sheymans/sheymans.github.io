Road shoesthe switch · wk 1–2

Where it settles. Mon and Wed on trail in the trail shoes; the Friday long run on the road in road shoes. The two tests (wk 6, wk 11) and the race-week runs are on the road too: flat, measurable, in the shoes you'll race in.

Why ease in at all. The surface is the bigger change, not the shoe. Road is harder, every stride lands the same way, and there are no climbs to walk, so the calf and Achilles take a repeated load they haven't had all year. Check the road shoe's heel drop: 8–12 mm, like the trail shoes, is a small change. A lower drop makes the Achilles work harder and needs a slower switch.

The steps. Wk 1: both runs on flat road in the road shoes (Wed 5k, Fri 8k). It's a recovery week, so flat and easy is what you want anyway. Wk 2: Mon and Wed back on trail, and Friday's 15k is the first long run on the road. Gate: only if the mornings after the wk-1 road runs were calm (calves, shins, Achilles). If not, run the wk-2 long run on trail and try the road again the next Friday. From wk 3 it's the settled pattern.

On the road. Take slightly shorter, quicker steps than on trail, and keep easy pace easy: the road feels faster, so the same effort comes out quicker. Log the road shoes' km and retire them past ~600 km.

WHAT PACE IS MINE?

Two tests set the goal: a 5k on Wed Dec 16 (wk 6) and a 10k on Wed Jan 20 (wk 11). The 10k counts more.
How it converts. MP = 10k pace × 1.13, or 5k pace × 1.20. That is a deliberately cautious conversion for three runs a week; the textbook ratios assume more mileage than this. Run the tests flat and evenly paced; a sprint finish doesn't count.

What to do with it. 15+ min inside the cutoff: race at MP and start with the pace team at or just under your finish. 5–15 min: run the first half 5 s/km slower than MP and walk only at aid stations. Under 5 min, or outside: tell me. Wednesdays get more MP work, the race becomes a run/walk, and the 6:30 pace team becomes the wall you stay ahead of.

BULLETPROOF THE RIGHT FOOT

Mid-portion Achilles · posterior tibialis · plantar fascia · continued from Joshua Tree · the FOOT block on Mon/Wed/Fri
Where you are. A 3×6 single-leg with 15 lb · E bent-knee holds 3×40s · C the band for the posterior tibialis, the tendon below the inner ankle. The layout stays the same, because it has kept you flare-free since the July re-entry: heavy on Monday, springy on Wednesday, holds only on long-run Friday. Never stack heavy calf work on long-run legs.

The ladder. A gets +5 lb about every four weeks, up to 30 lb by wk 12. E picks up a dumbbell in wk 5, then hands over to S, a heavy seated soleus raise, in wk 9. D grows from pogos to single-leg hops and hop-and-stick. Load only goes up after seven calm mornings in a row. A marathon asks far more of the soleus and the Achilles than a half, so this is the block where they have to get properly strong.

3×6 = 3 sets of 6 · each = per leg · tempo A 3-2-3 (up · hold · down) · S 2-1-3 · C 2-1-3 · rest 60–90 s.

The exercises — exactly how

ISO · Isometric calf hold Pain relief + tendon stiffness

ToePro, toes elevated. Rise about halfway up and hold dead still for the full count: no bouncing, no lowering. A real effort, pain ≤3/10. These calm an irritable tendon and prime it without fatigue. That's why they're the only foot work on long-run days and in race week.

A · Single-leg heel raise, toes elevated, loaded Achilles (mid-portion) + plantar fascia

Balls of the feet on the ToePro so the toes sit higher than the heels. Hold a wall for balance only, with the dumbbell in the hand on the working side. Rise (3 s), pause (2 s), lower on one leg (3 s) until the heel drops below the platform. Cue: rise straight up over the big toe and don't let the ankle roll outward (this protects the posterior tibialis). If the load makes the single-leg rise ugly, rise on two and lower on one. The slow single-leg lower is the part that counts.

E · Bent-knee calf hold Soleus · weeks 2–8

Same setup, knees bent ~30° the whole time, rise halfway and hold. The bent knee slackens the gastrocnemius and hands the work to the soleus: the muscle that fatigues late in a long race and protects the Achilles on descents. From wk 5, hold a 15 lb dumbbell.

S · Seated soleus raise, barbell (from wk 9) Soleus, heavy — replaces E

Sit on the bench, balls of the feet on the ToePro or a plate, knees at 90°. Lay the barbell across the thighs just above the knees, on a folded towel. Push up (2 s), hold (1 s), lower into the stretch (3 s). Start with the empty bar, 3×10. Add 5 kg when the last set feels like 7/10. The soleus takes several times your body weight on every stride, and this is the only exercise in the plan that loads it anywhere near that. That's why it arrives as the long runs pass 20k. Shoulder: roll the bar up from the floor onto your thighs. It never needs to go near the rack position.

C · Resisted inversion Posterior tibialis — below the inner ankle

Sit with the leg out and loop the band around the forefoot, anchored so it pulls the foot outward. Keep the heel still and turn the sole inward and down (2 s), then come back with control (3 s). Late in a marathon, a tired posterior tibialis lets the arch drop. That's what this prevents.

D · Pogos → hop-and-stick Elastic capacity — the tendon as a spring

Pogos: small, fast hops off the balls of the feet. Stiff ankles, minimal knee bend, quick contacts, like skipping rope. Double-leg first, then single-leg. Hop-and-stick (from wk 8): hop forward ~30–50 cm on one leg and land, then freeze for 2 s without the knee or arch caving in. This trains the landing, and 42 km is about 35,000 landings.

Pre-run ritual — ~7 min, before every run

1 · ISO primer. 2×40s calf holds right before heading out. Load dampens tendon pain for hours.
2 · Walk 5 minutes briskly before any running, outside in the morning light (it doubles as the light protocol below).
3 · First km flat and deliberately slow. Hills only once warm, uphills before downhills.
4 · Shoes. 8–12 mm drop, retired past ~600 km. This block is ~580 km on its own: start it in a fresh pair. Buy race shoes by mid-January, so they've done the wk-10 rehearsal and at least one more long run before Mar 7.
5 · No calf stretching before runs. The holds are the warm-up.
Daily check. Rate the first steps out of bed: calm / stiff / painful. A drift upward means hold the next progression, not stop.
Pain rule. Up to 5/10 during the exercise or run is OK if it's back to normal by the next morning and isn't getting worse week to week. If morning pain climbs, you limp, or it sharpens, drop back one level (less weight / fewer sets / shorter run). Don't push through. A marathon block forgives a lost week; it doesn't forgive a lost month.

Pacing

  • The first mile drops steeply. Brake and save the quads.
  • First 10k at MP + 10 s/km. The course rolls all the way, so don't bank time in the first six miles.
  • Hold MP to 30k, then use what's left.
  • Pace teams run 3:00 → 6:30 in 10-minute steps. Start with the one that matches your test, and stay well ahead of 6:30.

Fuel & drink

  • One 30 g gel every 30 min from minute 30 (~60 g/h, about 11 gels over six hours).
  • The course's gels at miles 12 and 18 are top-ups. Carry the rest.
  • Water and Electrolit every ~1.5 miles: walk 15–20 s, drink, go. That's the rhythm you rehearsed.

If the Achilles talks

  • Switch to run 4 min / walk 1 min and finish it that way.
  • The pain rule doesn't change on race day.
  • After: keep walking, eat within the hour, and walk only for the next week.
